# NUMPA Editorial and Publishing Guide

## 1. Editorial purpose

NUMPA content exists to help people solve real problems in data analysis, data cleaning, machine learning and practical AI/data learning. Publishing supports the services; it is not a separate growth activity disconnected from product value.

## 2. Content types

- **Blog:** explanatory articles, practical lessons and product-adjacent topics.
- **Tutorials:** step-by-step workflows with prerequisites, examples and expected outcomes.
- **Learning guides:** structured topic introductions and learning sequences.
- **Research notes:** evidence-led analysis with methodology, source links, dates and limitations.
- **Infographics:** concise visual explanations that remain understandable with accessible text alternatives.
- **Resources:** curated external datasets, documentation, tools and learning references.

## 3. Required article fields

Each published article should have:

- Unique slug and page title.
- Concise, accurate description.
- Topic/category.
- Actual author and reviewer where applicable.
- Original introduction and clear reader outcome.
- Structured headings and useful examples.
- Publication and last-updated dates.
- Source references for factual claims.
- Related internal links where genuinely useful.
- Accessibility text for meaningful images.
- Clear limitations, assumptions and disclosure of AI assistance where relevant.

Do not invent authors, credentials, dates, citations, benchmark results, search volumes or product capabilities.

## 4. Quality review

Before publication, check:

1. Does the page answer a specific reader question?
2. Is the content original, accurate and understandable?
3. Are claims supported by sources and correctly qualified?
4. Are examples safe, reproducible and free of personal data/secrets?
5. Are links valid and licences respected?
6. Is the page accessible and usable on mobile?
7. Are title, description, canonical and headings accurate?
8. Does the page connect naturally to a relevant NUMPA service without misleading claims?

## 5. SEO, AEO and GEO

Write for the reader first. Use descriptive titles, concise direct answers, meaningful headings, internal links and structured data only when it accurately describes visible content. Do not keyword-stuff, generate doorway pages or promise search rankings or AI citations.

## 6. Publishing workflow

1. Draft in the content registry or approved editorial source.
2. Add citations and verify claims.
3. Request editorial/technical review as needed.
4. Preview page rendering, accessibility and metadata.
5. Merge through the repository PR workflow.
6. Verify sitemap and crawlability.
7. Revisit time-sensitive pages and update or retire stale material.

## 7. Corrections and maintenance

When an error is identified, correct the page, preserve a clear update date and explain material corrections when readers could be affected. Remove broken, obsolete or unlicensed resources. Never silently change a research conclusion without updating its methodology and evidence.

## 8. Current limitations

The initial publishing system is code-backed and static. It does not yet provide a CMS, contributor submission form, author account portal, scheduled publishing, analytics dashboard or automated research agent. Do not advertise those capabilities until they are implemented and verified.
